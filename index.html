Frequently asked questions

The short version of everything people ask us. Anything missing? Ask away.

Operations Academia is the academic job-market hub for Operations Management, Operations Research and Analytics. Our vision is to make the search for an academic job in Operations (and related areas) more efficient and transparent — for PhD candidates, for recently hired faculty, for hiring committees, and for prospective students deciding whether to join the field.

In one scrolling page you will find the current academic job postings, this market’s candidates, the confirmed placements, and the first Job Market Survey ever conducted for candidates in our field. Everything is posted by verified members of the academic community, and using the site is free.

The site is supported by the MSOM Society of INFORMS, sponsored by the CUHK DOT Department, and was founded in 2014 by Konstantinos I. Stouras (University College Dublin), who runs it in his free time.

Sign in (with Google, ORCID, or an e-mail address and password), open Post a job and fill in the form. Your posting appears on the site automatically, normally within a few minutes, and stays up for the rest of the job-market year. Posting is free.

If you register with an e-mail address and a password, we first e-mail you a link to confirm that the address is yours, and nothing on the site works for the new account until you click it. Signing in with Google needs no extra step, since Google has already confirmed the address.

Because the posting is tied to your account, you can come back at any time to correct it, extend the deadline, or withdraw it — from My postings in your personal area. Only the fields marked shown publicly ever appear on the site; your e-mail address is never published.

If you are applying to academic jobs in Operations this season, sign in and create your job-market profile — with your CV attached, your research areas, and the day(s) you present at INFORMS, so hiring committees can find your talk and attend it.

Profiles are collected continuously but revealed all at once, at 14:00 UTC on a set date each season, which is morning in the Americas, afternoon in Europe and evening in East Asia, so no one gains from posting earlier or later. The note above the candidates list works out what that is on your own clock. Each candidate has one profile per market year — if you have already filed one this season, the form reopens it for editing. You can edit your profile, replace or remove its files (a replaced CV keeps only the newest version), or take it down at any time; your contact address is shown only if you opt in.

Yes, on your account page, and only you and the site’s maintainer can see it. It is drawn exactly as it will appear on the candidates page once every profile is revealed, and the form shows the same preview while you type.

Yes, at any time; the card then shows when it was last updated. Before the reveal your changes stay private and go public with everyone else’s at 14:00 UTC on the reveal date; after it they reach the candidates page within a few minutes of saving. Open Post your candidacy while signed in and it reopens your profile.

Once a candidate has accepted a position, anyone with an account can report the placement: who, from which PhD program, joining which institution and as what. It appears in the Confirmed placements list at the next update, and the person who reported it can correct or withdraw it later.

This record is what lets the whole community see, season after season, how supply met demand.

Continuously. Postings, profiles and placements are submitted by the community and publish automatically — job postings normally within a few minutes, placements within the hour, and candidate profiles all together at 14:00 UTC on the season’s reveal date. There is no third-party service between a poster and the page any more.

The lists always show the job-market year under way. A market year runs from 1 July to 30 June and is numbered by the year it ends — so the current market began on 1 July. Previous seasons stay in the archive.

Sign in and open E-mail alerts: you can be told about every new job posting, or only those matching filters you choose (institution, type, entry level, location…), reminded of the postings matching those filters that are closing in the seven days after each digest (daily or weekly, to see every closing date), and told about changes to the site itself and, if you like, about this season’s candidate profiles once they are revealed. You pick the frequency, and every message carries an unsubscribe link. Quicker still: narrow the list on the job postings page and press Save as e-mail alert, beside the Excel download, and the alerts page opens with those filters already filled in.

The site carries what the community posts, and after a decade that is most of the widely advertised academic openings in the field. But coverage ultimately depends on schools posting — so if your department’s opening is missing, post it (it takes a few minutes and is free), or send the link to the search committee chair.

Yes. The site has an anonymous forum in two rooms. The Candidates’ room is for the accounts holding a candidate profile for the season under way; the Open forum is for every registered account with a confirmed e-mail address, faculty included. You post under a random handle drawn for the season, the same in both rooms and never your name; threads carry tags, replies can quote a passage of an earlier post, and a post can be liked or disliked. Sign in and open Forum from your account menu. The forum guide is pinned at the top of each room; read it once before your first post.

Accounts exist so that we know where a posting comes from and can reach its author, nothing more. The one thing about registered accounts that is public is how many there are: the front page shows the count and the analytics page shows how it has grown, and neither says who they are.

About

A community effort, since 2014

For years the academic job market in Operations ran on paywalled forums, forwarded emails, and word of mouth. Even candidates who paid for access ended up building their own spreadsheets to track where to apply, and deadlines and required materials were easy to miss. Search committees, meanwhile, saw only the candidates who applied to them, and departments not hiring in a given year had no practical way to follow the work coming out of new PhDs. Operations Academia was built in 2014 to close that gap, following the example of the job market platforms already established in Economics and Marketing.

The site brings postings, candidate profiles, and records from past markets into one public archive, updated each season and open to everyone. It has since been used by more than 200 universities worldwide, and its placement data has become a shared reference point for departments planning searches and for candidates judging where they stand. It is supported by the MSOM Society of INFORMS and currently sponsored by the CUHK Department of Decisions, Operations and Technology.

Konstantinos I. Stouras is Assistant Professor of Operations Management at UCD, Michael Smurfit Graduate School of Business and holds a PhD from INSEAD. His research covers innovation, contest design, and the organizational effects of AI. He founded Operations Academia in 2014 and continues to run it in his free time. More at stouras.com.
